# Escalation Rules

Escalation rules define when a case should be routed to government emergency authorities, official decision-making bodies, NGO coordinators, professional supporters, partner organizations, safety reviewers, or technical maintainers.

OpenRelief is designed to support humanitarian information work. It does not replace official emergency response systems, government decision-making, rescue command structures, medical services, or law enforcement.

## Escalate to Government or Official Emergency Authorities

Cases should be escalated to relevant government decision-making bodies, emergency management departments, public safety authorities, official rescue command centers, or other competent authorities when they involve:

* life-threatening or time-critical emergencies;
* mass-casualty incidents, large-scale displacement, trapped persons, missing persons, or urgent rescue needs;
* floods, earthquakes, landslides, typhoons, fires, explosions, epidemics, infrastructure failures, or other public emergencies requiring official coordination;
* evacuation, shelter, medical transport, road access, power, water, communications, or other critical infrastructure issues;
* requests that require official rescue dispatch, public safety intervention, medical emergency response, or cross-agency coordination;
* clustered reports indicating a possible emerging disaster, secondary hazard, or rapidly worsening situation;
* information that may affect official public warnings, evacuation decisions, resource allocation, or emergency command decisions;
* sensitive geospatial information, casualty information, or field reports that should be verified by competent authorities before public release.

Where immediate danger is present, responders should follow local emergency reporting procedures and contact the appropriate official emergency channels first.

## Escalate to NGO Coordinator

Cases should be escalated to an NGO coordinator when they involve:

* urgent or critical help requests that require humanitarian coordination;
* unclear ownership or no obvious responsible team;
* multiple NGOs, volunteer groups, community responders, or partner organizations may be involved;
* resource gaps that cannot be matched automatically;
* conflicting reports that require human judgment;
* repeated requests from the same affected area;
* coordination between public help requests, resource offers, field information, and follow-up actions;
* cases where official emergency response has been notified but humanitarian support is still needed.

## Escalate to Medical or Professional Support

Cases should be escalated to medical or professional support when they involve:

* injury, illness, pregnancy, chronic disease, oxygen, medication, mental health crisis, disability, elderly care, child protection, or other care needs;
* requests for medical, psychological, legal, social work, rescue, or protection-related advice;
* risk of unsafe non-professional intervention;
* uncertainty about whether a case requires professional assessment.

AI-generated medical, psychological, legal, rescue, or protection-related suggestions must not be treated as final advice.

## Escalate to Safety Reviewer

Cases should be escalated to a safety reviewer when they involve:

* personal data exposure;
* child or vulnerable-person information;
* household details, medical conditions, contact information, or precise sensitive locations;
* public maps, reports, or briefs that may reveal sensitive details;
* AI output suggesting unsafe, discriminatory, misleading, or unsupported action;
* possible abuse, fraud, coercion, exploitation, harassment, or security risk;
* uncertainty about whether information should be published, restricted, anonymized, or removed.

## Escalate to Technical Maintainer

Cases should be escalated to a technical maintainer when they involve:

* system errors affecting intake, review, notification, routing, mapping, reporting, or audit;
* possible data leak, unauthorized access, permission failure, or privacy incident;
* prompt or model behavior creating systematic safety risks;
* evaluation failure in high-risk cases;
* broken integrations with reporting, notification, mapping, or coordination tools.

## Do Not Escalate Publicly

Sensitive cases should not be escalated through public GitHub issues, public chat channels, or public social media threads.

Cases involving personal data, precise locations, medical details, safety threats, vulnerable persons, rescue access, or official emergency coordination should be handled through restricted, appropriate, and accountable channels.

When information is incomplete, conflicting, outdated, or unverified, OpenRelief should label it clearly instead of presenting it as confirmed fact.
